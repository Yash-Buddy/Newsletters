Where it starts: the Pacific off South America

El Niño begins when trade winds weaken and warm water pools along the Peru and Ecuador coast. The heat then spreads to the global south.

  1. 1. Trade winds weakenWarm surface water that normally sits in the far west Pacific slides east.
  2. 2. Coast heats upThe Niño 1+2 zone off Peru and Ecuador warms first. Fish stocks fall and coastal rain rises.
  3. 3. Rain belts shiftRain moves east. Amazon, Colombia and Venezuela turn dry, while southern Brazil and Uruguay turn wet.
  4. 4. Effects travel west and eastThe shifted air flow brings drought, fires and weak monsoons to Asia, Australia and Africa in the months after.
